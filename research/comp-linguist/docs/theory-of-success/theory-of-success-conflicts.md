# Theory of Success: Conflicts

**Author:** Computational Linguist (AI Triad Research). **Date:** 2026-08-26.

## What a Conflict is

A Conflict is a structured disagreement between positions in the taxonomy: a place where nodes or claims contradict, weaken, or stand in tension with one another. Where a Crux names the question two camps diverge on, a Conflict is the concrete graph of opposing positions and the attack and support relations between them. Conflicts are grouped into clusters (Cluster 1, Cluster 2, and so on, with a member count each), so the corpus of thousands of individual oppositions is organized into a browsable set of related tensions.

Structure (as seen in the Conflicts view and the conflict tooling):
- **Clusters:** related conflicts grouped together, each with a size (for example Cluster 1 with over a thousand members).
- **Per-conflict detail:** the linked taxonomy nodes on each side, related conflicts, and the concrete instances that realize the conflict.
- **Typed edges:** the oppositions are edges with types (CONTRADICTS, WEAKENS, TENSION_WITH) and escalation weights.
- **QBAF analysis:** the conflict is analyzed as a bipolar argumentation graph so the system can reason about which positions withstand attack.

## The theory of success

A Conflict makes the taxonomy's internal tensions explicit and analyzable instead of latent. Two positions can sit in the taxonomy for a long time without anyone noticing they contradict; a Conflict surfaces that opposition, types it, and (through quantitative bipolar argumentation) lets the system reason about which side holds up under attack and where a tension is genuinely irreconcilable. Conflicts are how disagreement is made structural rather than anecdotal.

**A Conflict succeeds when it captures a real contradiction rather than a spurious edge, is clustered coherently with genuinely related conflicts, carries correctly typed and weighted opposition edges, and yields a meaningful, stable argument-acceptability under QBAF analysis.** The corpus succeeds when conflicts are deduplicated and clustered without fragmenting real groups or merging unrelated ones, grounded in actual edges, and analyzed with calibrated rather than merely asserted parameters.

Success criteria, each checkable:
1. **Genuine contradiction.** The opposition is real, not a false edge produced by loose matching.
2. **Coherent clustering.** Related conflicts group into one cluster; a single real tension is not fragmented across several.
3. **Correct edge typing and weight.** CONTRADICTS / WEAKENS / TENSION_WITH are applied correctly, with defensible escalation weights.
4. **Meaningful, stable QBAF acceptability.** Gradual bipolar analysis produces argument strengths that are interpretable and stable, with oscillation controlled by calibrated damping.
5. **Grounding.** Linked nodes, related conflicts, and concrete instances are present.

Failure modes, each an active concern:
- **Spurious conflicts:** a false contradiction generated by over-eager edge detection.
- **Mis-clustering:** unrelated conflicts lumped together, or one real tension scattered across clusters.
- **Wrong edge typing or weights:** the attack weights (1.0 / 1.1 / 1.2 for rebut / undercut / undermine) are currently stipulated, not validated.
- **QBAF instability:** oscillation in the acceptability computation, which the damping parameter exists to control.
- **Duplication:** the same conflict represented multiple times outside its cluster.

## How Conflicts are generated

- **From typed edges.** Conflicts are derived from the opposition edges between taxonomy nodes (CONTRADICTS, WEAKENS, TENSION_WITH). Enrichment and consolidation passes (`enrich_conflicts_qbaf.py`, `consolidate_conflicts.py`) turn raw oppositions into consolidated conflicts and group them into clusters.
- **QBAF analysis.** `Invoke-QbafConflictAnalysis` treats each conflict as an edge-weighted quantitative bipolar argumentation framework and computes argument acceptability via gradual semantics. The damping level (`qbaf_damping_level`, marked derived in the provenance register) was raised in response to observed oscillation telemetry (`qbaf_oscillation_*`).
- **Clustering and consolidation.** Related conflicts are grouped so the view presents a set of coherent tensions rather than thousands of loose edges.

## How Conflicts are used

- **Surfacing internal tensions.** The Conflicts view organizes the corpus into clusters and shows, per conflict, the opposing nodes, related conflicts, and instances.
- **Argument acceptability.** The QBAF analysis lets the system reason about which positions withstand attack, giving a structured, inspectable read on a disagreement rather than an LLM verdict alone.
- **Linkage.** Situations and cruxes reference conflicts (via `conflict_ids`), so a scenario or disagreement can point at the structural tension it involves.
- **Debate grounding.** Conflicts are the structured disagreements a debate can engage, connecting the abstract taxonomy to the live argument.

## Success metrics and current gaps

- **Stability:** `qbaf_oscillation_*` telemetry, with `qbaf_damping_level` (derived) tuned to keep the acceptability computation stable.
- **Open items:** the `attack_weights` (1.0 / 1.1 / 1.2) are stipulated and unvalidated. The Gradual AA-CBR review (t/3023) recommends this exact module as the place to adopt a principled, differentiable gradual semantics and, if a labeled outcome set is ever built, gradient-learned edge weights, which would take these parameters off stipulated.
